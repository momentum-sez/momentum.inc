The Thesis

The built environment is the largest asset class humans maintain, yet its ownership rails are archaic: high friction, low transparency, rentier capture.

Archai turns land and buildings into programmable, arbitration-enabled institutions — where people enter by committing capital, earn ownership over time, and surplus flows by explicit constitutional logic. Compliant, auditable, and jurisdiction-portable through Mass primitives.

Why Now: The Enabling Stack

Mass makes entities, ownership, and consent programmable — the legal substrate. HomeOS makes buildings continuously measurable — the operational substrate. EZ Stack makes jurisdictional portability possible. Together: a real programmable institution, not paper deeds with a digital veneer.

The Problem

Ownership is structurally exclusionary

Entry requires large down payments, creditworthiness, and legal sophistication. Exit is illiquid and expensive.

Rentier dynamics dominate housing markets

Default incentives reward holding scarce assets, restricting supply, and harvesting rent. Abundant, community-aligned housing requires different incentive architecture.

Buildings are opaque, so risk is mispriced

HomeOS flips this: buildings self-report occupancy, energy, maintenance, and revenue — the continuous operational evidence that programmable ownership requires.

The coordination problem

Land ownership encodes governance, cultural norms, and collective investment decisions. Archai expresses these as programmable institutional logic.

Design Principles

A programmable institution. Must obey institutional physics.

Legal reality first

Every vault asset pairs with a real legal wrapper (SPV, trust, foundation, coop) holding title.

Yield first, not appreciation first

Built assets as cash-flow machines. Yield is the design target.

Anti-speculation must be engineered

Pricing rules, transfer constraints, premium capture, exit gates, governance limits.

Composability at the primitive level

Mass provides primitives. Archai composes them into built-asset institutions — the programmable-ownership layer for HomeOS buildings.

Continuous attestations, not annual audits

Cryptographically secured attestation streams. Compliance verified programmatically without exposing private data.

Governance with hard guardrails

Trustees, emergency powers, upgrade controls. Decentralization earned, not assumed.

Vault Economics

The vault balance sheet

Capital stack:

  • Senior capital: bank debt/bonds, capped return, first claim
  • Resident stake: time-locked, compensated via rent discounts + earned equity
  • Programmatic equity: variable return, residual surplus
  • Sponsor/operator equity: absorbs early risk
  • Commons stake: protocol fee, insurance backstops

Two primary stake products

A) Resident Stake (stake-to-live)

Stake capital for 3–7 years. Receive rent discounts, earned equity, scoped governance, principal redemption at maturity.

B) Capital Stake (stake-to-build)

Non-resident capital with defined term, coupon-like distributions, potential equity kicker, no occupancy rights. Scales supply without speculation.

Earned equity: from first principles

Accrual = f(stake, term, risk multiplier, contribution score, policy) — computed against vault NAV.

Surplus: the sacred object

Surplus = NOI − Debt Service − Reserves. Allocated by policy:

  • Resident/staker distributions: 50–70%
  • Reinvestment: 20–40%
  • Protocol fee to Commons: 5–10%
  • Insurance/contingency buffer

Surplus allocation is the economic constitution.

Liquidity & Exit

Three exit paths (each with constraints)

1. Redemption at maturity. Principal less fees, subject to solvency and redemption queues.

2. Secondary transfer. Whitelisted recipients only. Premium cap relative to NAV; excess routed to Commons.

3. Cross-vault portability. Stake exits one vault and enters another under rules, enabling mobility without liquidation cascades.

Redemption gates: preventing bank runs

Max redemption per epoch, variable queue times tied to liquidity, emergency halts when reserves breach threshold.

"Non-speculative" as a pricing rule

NAV-bounded: tight band, excess premium taxed. Affordability-first: occupancy non-transferable or at-cost. Growth: capped appreciation with commons capture.

Governance & Control Surfaces

Polycentric governance

Resident Council — community rules, service priorities, budget. Weight: occupancy + contribution.

Capital Council — leverage, reserves, capex, issuance. Weight: stake and ownership (bounded).

Trustee/Guardian — legal compliance, emergency actions, constitutional enforcement.

Constitutional constraints (hard-coded)

Leverage caps, reserve floors, affordability constraints, disclosure requirements. Upgrade-resistant by design.

Evidence-based governance

Proposals reference attested HomeOS metrics, audited financials, and risk reports.

Legal & Compliance

The Mass integration is not optional

SPV/trust is a Mass entity. Stake positions are Mass-native ownership. Transfers governed by identity gates and consent workflows. Distributions use Mass fiscal rails.

Assets carry legal modules

Each vault declares its jurisdictional module. Transfers compute the intersection of permissible actions. The asset enforces constraints programmatically.

Attestation streams are the regulator interface

Verifiable compliance without exposing private data: holder eligibility, transfer compliance, treasury flows, operating metrics integrity.

Jurisdiction SDK

Deployable legal + compliance modules per jurisdiction. Vaults instantiate within a chosen module. Upgradeable with governance and trustee oversight.

Security, Risk & Solvency

Threat model

Title defects, operator fraud, valuation manipulation, data-feed corruption, governance capture, liquidity cascades, jurisdictional intervention, banking rail failure, executable contract bugs.

Controls

  • Segregated accounts (operating vs reserves vs distributions)
  • Independent appraisal + HomeOS attested ops data feeding NAV
  • Redemption gates + queues
  • Role-based access control
  • Operational + legal audit trails
  • Emergency guardian powers with transparency constraints
  • Insurance module (protocol-level underwriting)

Insurance and underwriting

Commons Treasury as first-loss backstop. Underwriting capital committed into risk-weighted pools. Vaults pay risk-based premiums from surplus. More vaults mean better risk modeling and cheaper capital.

Instrument Model

Two instrument categories

Vault Assets: stake positions, ownership units, use rights. Per-vault, jurisdiction-dependent.

Network instrument: protocol governance, underwriting collateral, commons fee capture.

Distribution design principles

  • Incentivize early vault bootstrapping, not permanent subsidy
  • Reward verified contribution over passive speculation
  • Fund long-term commons
  • No short-term issuance schedules

Regulatory posture

Vault assets are regulated instruments; compliance embedded via Mass. The network instrument serves as governance weight and underwriting collateral. Jurisdiction-modular.

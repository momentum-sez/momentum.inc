The Position

The fund that writes the rules its portfolio operates under

Momentum designs the regulatory environments in which its portfolio companies operate. Broad Group needs building codes across the network. Momentum writes them. EHang needs multi-jurisdictional eVTOL approval. Momentum designs the passporting. The regulatory infrastructure goes in first. The capital follows.

Governments do not want software. They want jobs, revenue, and industrial capacity. Each zone receives a factory employing hundreds (Broad Group, $86M annual revenue per production line) and a transport hub connecting cities (EHang). Portfolio companies anchor each zone. New zones open corridors to every existing zone on the network.

The Structural Gap

Intelligence without institutions. Institutions without intelligence.

AI can analyze every jurisdiction on earth in seconds. It cannot incorporate a company in any of them. The institutions that govern economic life — registries, compliance, banking, identity — were built for human speed and walled-garden jurisdictions. $270 billion is spent annually on compliance that is manual, periodic, and adversarial.

Programmable institutions close this gap. Incorporation, compliance, banking, identity, and consent exposed as APIs, accessed by humans and AI agents through the same interface. A company incorporates under ADGM law, custodies assets through Hong Kong, holds a Delaware entity structure with Cayman fund administration, and moves between jurisdictions at machine speed. Thirteen jurisdictions are live.

Network Economics

Every zone multiplies every other zone

A company in Kazakhstan needs to bank in Singapore. Today that takes six months, a quarter million dollars, and a compliance team that speaks both regulatory languages. On the network, it routes through ADGM in seconds. Compliance re-evaluates at every border. The company pays less. The jurisdictions earn more. Momentum captures a fee at every hop.

Defensibility

Compounding trust, combinatorial cost of entry

Momentum owns the machine-executable model of how 210 jurisdictions interact. Every entity on Mass is a multi-harbored entity — an entity that can harbor in multiple jurisdictions simultaneously, with the strictest applicable rule worked out automatically across every compliance domain and jurisdiction it touches. How those rules compose is learned from operational reality — which thresholds actually trigger review, what evidence regulators accept, where formal rules diverge from practice.

Trust takes 18–36 months per government partnership. Thirteen jurisdictions, each negotiated individually. A full-stack charter city deployed at Próspera. Portfolio companies physically inside every zone: Broad Group factories, EHang transport hubs. Hardware you cannot replicate with a codebase. Each zone added opens compliance paths to every other zone on the network.

The architecture and research are published.

Timing

Three conditions that did not previously coexist

AI agents need institutional surfaces at machine speed. This demand did not exist three years ago. Which jurisdictions accept which entity types, how compliance domains interact across borders, where corridors exist and what they cost. Only the infrastructure that processes real operations knows.

Pakistan published its SIFC digital infrastructure mandate. Kazakhstan passed enabling legislation for Alatau City. Dubai FZC is coordinating 27 free zones under a unified digital architecture. Mass delivers real-time cross-jurisdictional compliance across 13+ live jurisdictions.

Hub positioning is irreversible. Governments will not permit two competing operating-system providers for their economy. Latecomers build parallel infrastructure or join the existing network on the operator’s terms. Each government partnership requires 18–36 months, non-parallelizable.

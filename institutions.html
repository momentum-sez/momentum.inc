Government Partnerships

The government partnership model

Governments want a co-architect, not a vendor. The model is platform-plus-merchant. The platform does not share its processing fee with the merchant; the merchant benefits because the platform makes its own customers more successful. Government statutory fees stay intact. Platform fees are a separate stream.

Zero upfront cost. Momentum covers all deployment costs during the 90-day deployment. The government’s only obligation is staff time for integration working sessions.

Existing revenue preserved. Government retains 100% of its existing statutory fee base. The platform does not reduce, redirect, or touch government fees.

Volume is the value. Consumers pay less total than they do today. Lower costs attract more entities. More entities, more statutory fees.

Government builds on top. Full API access to build new digital services: building permits, VASP compliance, land registry, professional licensing. Revenue from government-built services is shared (majority to government). Every government-built service increases platform transaction volume.

Sovereign exit clause. Government retains full documentation and transferable infrastructure at Day 90. No lock-in.

Revenue Architecture

Three layers of revenue

Layer 1 — Protocol Fees
Mass Ltd

Settlement, corridor-routing, and issuance fees, disclosed at corridor accession and earned by the protocol across every jurisdiction.

Layer 2 — Processing Fees
Local Operating Company

Consumer-facing processing fees on entity formation, license renewals, compliance filings, ownership transfers, banking, payments, FX. Revenue = consumer fees net of the protocol fee.

Layer 3 — Government Services
Revenue Share (Only Layer)

Government builds new digital services on platform APIs: building permits, VASP compliance, land registry. Revenue split on government-built services only (majority to government).

Each government partnership requires 18–36 months of non-parallelizable negotiation and deployment. Once embedded, switching costs compound with every entity formed and every corridor activated. BVI has 400,000 active IBCs, each renewing annually.

Economics

Seven fee layers per corridor, hub-and-spoke topology

Seven fee layers per corridor: entity formation, licensing, banking, payments, FX, lending, marketplace services. Hub zones (Delaware, Singapore, Switzerland, ADGM) anchor the network; spokes reach other jurisdictions through hub corridors. Network value scales Odlyzko O(n log n) — weakly superlinear, concentrated at hubs. Binding constraint on corridor formation is sovereign negotiation (~$1M, 2–4 years, 20–30% failure rate per corridor), not software.

Per-zone deployment economics: $3–9M fully-loaded Year-0 outlay produces $10–31M Mass-platform Year-3 ARR at the median zone, ramping to $40–120M at a DIFC-class mature zone. Portfolio companies operating inside each zone generate additional attribution, reported separately from platform revenue.

Distribution

Once the stack is embedded, it becomes the default for every company and every flow inside the zone. Trust cannot be purchased (18–36 months per jurisdiction).

Governments that fork the code discover they need the network. The institutional world model is built from operational data across US, BVI, Cayman, Honduras, Hong Kong, Singapore, and UAE — on a trajectory toward 1,000+ entities (illustrative target). Code without it is an empty schema.

Corridor formation is bilateral between sovereigns. A new zone activates corridors with k partners at software cost O(k); each of those corridors is a 2–4 year sovereign negotiation (~$1M per corridor, 20–30% formation failure rate). Network value concentrates at hub zones — Delaware, Singapore, Switzerland, ADGM — where spokes compose institutional state. Ten-year realistic trajectory: 5–8 hubs, 20–30 spokes, 10–20 new corridors per year at maturity, 3–6× the base rate of bilateral treaty formation.
